MATHS 104 Conics and Differential Equations. Lecture notes in three parts: conic sections (parts 1 and 2), then parametric equations and differential equations (part 3). Teach every derivation step by step and use the lecture examples (or close variants) as worked examples placed right after the idea they use.

LESSON 1: Introduction to Conic Sections
What a conic section is: the curve made when a plane cuts a double cone. The four types: circle, ellipse, parabola, hyperbola, and how the angle of the cutting plane decides which one appears. The general second-degree equation Ax^2 + Bxy + Cy^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0 and how the discriminant (the notes use delta = 4AC - B^2) identifies the conic: ellipse or circle when positive (circle if A = C and B = 0), parabola when zero, hyperbola when negative. Real-life uses of each curve.

LESSON 2: The Circle: Standard Equation
Circle as all points at a fixed distance r from a centre (h, k). Derive (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2 from the distance formula, and x^2 + y^2 = r^2 for a centre at the origin. Lecture examples: centre (3, 4) and radius 5; centre (4, -1) and radius 6; centre (-2, -3) passing through (2, -1).

LESSON 3: The Circle: General Form
General form x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0, centre (-g, -f), radius sqrt(g^2 + f^2 - c), and when it is a real circle. Converting between standard and general form by completing the square. Lecture example: show that 9x^2 + 9y^2 - 18x + 36y - 45 = 0 is a circle and find its centre and radius.

LESSON 4: Finding the Equation of a Circle
How many conditions fix a circle. Equation from centre and a point, from three points, from the end points of a diameter using (x - x1)(x - x2) + (y - y1)(y - y2) = 0 with the reason it works, and from other conditions. Lecture example: diameter with end points M(-2, 1) and N(3, 5).

LESSON 5: Tangent and Normal to a Circle
Tangent as a line touching the circle at one point and normal as the perpendicular to it. Gradient by implicit differentiation, equation of the tangent and the normal at a given point. Lecture example: tangent and normal to 2x^2 + 2y^2 - 2x - 5y + 3 = 0 at (1, 1).

LESSON 6: The Parabola: Definition and Standard Equations
Parabola as all points equally far from a focus and a directrix. Derive y^2 = 4ax with vertex, focus (a, 0), directrix x = -a, axis, latus rectum of length 4a with end points (a, 2a) and (a, -2a). The other three orientations: y^2 = -4ax, x^2 = 4ay, x^2 = -4ay. Lecture example: find the focus, directrix and axis of y^2 = -16x.

LESSON 7: The Parabola: Finding Equations
Equation from focus and directrix, from the vertex and a point, and with the vertex away from the origin, (y - k)^2 = 4a(x - h). Distance from a point to a line for an oblique directrix. Lecture examples: vertex at the origin, axis along the y-axis and passing through (8, -4); focus (3, 2) with directrix x - 5y + 4 = 0, giving the general equation.

LESSON 8: Tangent and Normal to a Parabola
Gradient of a parabola by differentiation, tangent and normal at the point (x1, y1) on y^2 = 4ax. Lecture examples: tangent and normal to y^2 = 16x at one end of the latus rectum; tangent and normal to x^2 + x - 3y + 4 = 0 at the point (2, 3) using implicit differentiation.

LESSON 9: The Ellipse: Definition, Equation and Properties
Ellipse as all points whose distances to two foci add up to a constant 2a. Full derivation of x^2/a^2 + y^2/b^2 = 1 with b^2 = a^2 - c^2. Vertices, co-vertices, major and minor axes, foci (+-c, 0), latus rectum of length 2b^2/a with end points (c, +-b^2/a), and the form with the major axis along the y-axis. Lecture examples: major axis 20 and minor axis 12, find the foci; vertices (0, +-10) and foci (0, +-6), find the equation.

LESSON 10: The Ellipse: Eccentricity and Finding Equations
Eccentricity e = c/a with 0 < e < 1 and what it says about the shape, directrices x = +-a/e, and an ellipse with centre (h, k). Lecture examples: vertices (3, -1) and (3, 7) with e = 1/4, centre (3, 3); sketch and analyse 16x^2 + 36y^2 = 576 with a = 6, b = 4, c = sqrt(20), about 4.472, and latus rectum about 5.333.

LESSON 11: The Hyperbola: Definition, Equation and Asymptotes
Hyperbola as all points where the difference of the distances to two foci is the constant 2a. Full derivation of x^2/a^2 - y^2/b^2 = 1 with b^2 = c^2 - a^2, transverse and conjugate axes, vertices (+-a, 0), foci (+-c, 0) with c > a, and asymptotes y = +-(b/a)x. Lecture example: foci (+-5, 0) and a vertex at (3, 0), giving x^2/9 - y^2/16 = 1.

LESSON 12: The Hyperbola: Eccentricity, Directrices and Finding Equations
Eccentricity e = c/a > 1, directrices x = +-a^2/c, latus rectum 2b^2/a, and a hyperbola with centre (h, k). Lecture examples: foci (7, 3) and (-3, 3) with e = 2, centre (2, 3), a = 5/2 and b^2 = 75/4; sketch and analyse x^2/16 - y^2/25 = 1 with a = 4, b = 5, c = sqrt(41), about 6.4, asymptotes y = +-(5/4)x and latus rectum 12.5.

LESSON 13: Parametric Equations: Meaning and Graphs
What a parameter is, writing x and y both in terms of t, building a table of points from values of t, and showing the direction of motion along the curve. Lecture examples: x = 2t + 1 and y = t^2; circular motion x = 5 cos t and y = 5 sin t, with applications of circular parametric equations.

LESSON 14: Eliminating the Parameter
The general method: solve one equation for t and substitute into the other. Linear case and the trigonometric method using sin^2 t + cos^2 t = 1. Lecture examples: x = 5t - 4 and y = 2t + 3; x = 4 cos t and y = 4 sin t, a circle; x = 6 cos t and y = 3 sin t, an ellipse. Link back to the conic equations.

LESSON 15: Parametric Equations of Motion and Rates of Change
Displacement s(t), velocity v = ds/dt and acceleration a = dv/dt and how they connect. Rate of change in business, including marginal cost. Lecture examples: s = 4t^3 + 5t^2 + 2 metres, find the velocity function and the velocity at t = 3; v = 6t^2 - 4t + 10, find the acceleration at t = 5; s = 5t^3 with v = 15t^2 and a = 30t; cost C(x) = 500 + 20x + 0.5x^2, find the marginal cost C'(x) = 20 + x.

LESSON 16: Introduction to Differential Equations
What a differential equation is, order of an equation (first order and second order examples), the basic idea of solving by integration, general and particular solutions, and initial conditions.

LESSON 17: Separable Differential Equations
Separating the variables, integrating both sides, and including the constant. Lecture examples: dy/dx = xy; dy/dx = 3xy with y = 2 when x = 0 using the initial condition.

LESSON 18: First-Order Linear Equations and the Integrating Factor
The form dy/dx + P(x) y = Q(x), the integrating factor IF = e^(integral of P(x) dx), and the steps of the method. Lecture example: dy/dx + 3y = 6 with P(x) = 3.
